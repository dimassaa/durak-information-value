The endgame rule, and why it distorts every rate here

The win check runs the moment a card leaves the hand, so an attacker playing a last card wins outright and the defender never gets to answer. On this dataset most games end that way. This is spec 3.4's documented rule set rather than a defect, but it compresses the endgame: a 'close' game here is closer than a reader of the number would assume.

A positional effect can be told from a dealing effect here

The two seat counts are near-equal, so a rate difference between two strategies is about the strategies and not about which seat they were dealt into. The field that answers the positional question is <b>first_attacker_wins</b>, which counts whoever attacked first: under RULE_DERIVED the first attacker is chosen by the rules and is not seat 0, so it cannot be recovered from the seat counts. It reads 0.48 on the primary dataset -- the first attacker loses about half again as often as it wins, which a clean 50/50 seat split cannot show.
